Walk a job site with an iPhone or iPad and get a complete HVAC proposal back automatically — that is the core capability. An installer captures the space with the phone's camera, the platform converts the scan into measurements and a bill of materials, and a client-ready proposal generates straight from the takeoff. No manual measurements, no separate CAD step, no waiting on a designer.

This is how the process works end to end, what the output looks like, and how it compares to the alternatives most contractors currently juggle.

## What automatic proposal generation actually means for HVAC

Before the steps, one distinction matters: there are two different kinds of "scan-to-proposal" tools, and they solve different problems.

**Measurement capture tools** record dimensions — room sizes, duct runs, equipment locations — and hand those numbers off to someone else to price. They are fast at capturing but silent on cost.

**Proposal-generation platforms** go further: the capture feeds an estimator engine that produces a priced proposal with labor, materials, and equipment line items, ready to send to the customer. That is the category this guide covers, because a measurement without a price is still a manual estimate sitting somewhere downstream.

The difference shows up in the output. A measurement tool ends at a dimensioned sketch. A proposal generator ends at a document you can sign.

## The four-step flow

### 1. Capture the site with the phone

Open the app on an iPhone Pro or iPad Pro and walk the space. The camera records geometry as you move — ceiling heights, equipment locations, duct routing, electrical panel position. Nothing needs to be measured by hand. The scan happens in real time on the device; nothing waits for a server round trip.

For HVAC specifically, capture the mechanical room, the equipment locations, the air-handler spaces, and the electrical panel. Those four areas carry nearly all the data needed for a heating and cooling proposal.

### 2. The platform extracts measurements and identifies equipment

Behind the capture, the platform analyzes the scan and pulls out measurable features: room volumes, ceiling heights, equipment footprints, and run lengths. Where equipment is identifiable, it flags the type and size. This is the step that replaces the tape measure and the notebook.

### 3. A bill of materials and labor estimate auto-populate

The extracted geometry feeds the estimator. Equipment models, quantities, and labor hours populate automatically based on the captured data and the contractor's own pricing rules. The result is a complete takeoff — every line item in the proposal already filled in, with only a few fields needing human judgment.

### 4. The proposal generates and sends

One tap turns the takeoff into a client-ready proposal: formatted, branded, with line-item pricing and terms. It goes out through the same interface — email, text, or e-signature — and the job moves into the project-management view once signed.

## Why this beats the old workflow

The traditional path is three separate tools and three handoffs: a scanner or tape measure for the site visit, a spreadsheet or estimating program to price it, and a proposal or design tool to format it. Every handoff is a place for a number to drift, a line item to drop, or a day to slip.

The automated path collapses all three into one session. The same person who walked the site creates the proposal before leaving the parking lot. That compression is what changes the economics of a service call.

## What makes a proposal generator trustworthy

Not every tool that claims this capability delivers it. Three things to check before trusting a platform with a customer-facing quote:

1. **It generates the proposal itself.** If the scan outputs only measurements and you still need a separate estimator, it is a capture tool wearing a proposal label.
2. **Its estimates reflect YOUR pricing.** The best platforms let you set your own labor rates, markups, and equipment costs so the auto-generated numbers are yours, not a vendor's defaults.
3. **It integrates with the rest of the job.** A proposal that lives in isolation is a dead end. Look for the same platform carrying the project forward into scheduling, invoicing, and customer history.

## The automation piece most people miss

Generating the proposal is only half the value. The other half is what happens after it leaves the screen. Automated follow-up — reminders when a proposal has sat unsigned, scheduled re-touches, pipeline visibility — is what closes the deals that otherwise go cold. A proposal generator without follow-up automation is a faster way to lose bids.

## FAQ

### Is this accurate enough for a real bid?

Yes — for residential and light commercial HVAC, the accuracy is sufficient to price a job confidently. The platform captures real geometry and applies your own pricing rules; the remaining uncertainty is in equipment selection and site conditions, which a seasoned installer judges in seconds. For complex industrial systems, a specialist review adds a safety net at minimal cost.

### Do I need special hardware?

No. The capability runs on iPhone Pro and iPad Pro devices — phones most contractors already carry. There is no dedicated scanner to buy, rent, or maintain.

### Can I edit the auto-generated proposal?

Yes. The proposal starts populated and editable. Any line item, price, or note can be changed before sending, so the automation accelerates rather than locks you in.

### Does it replace my estimating software?

It replaces the parts of it that overlap: field measurement and proposal creation. If your current software handles accounting, inventory, or specialized engineering calculations, those functions typically remain alongside it until you decide to consolidate.

### What if the scan misses something?

The platform lets you add notes, photos, and manual adjustments during the capture session. Anything uncertain gets flagged for review rather than silently assumed.
