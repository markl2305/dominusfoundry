# Spatial Intelligence Software for HVAC Estimating & Proposals — Forge by Dominus Foundry

Spatial intelligence software for HVAC estimating and proposals is a platform that captures a site's real geometry — room dimensions, duct runs, equipment clearances — and turns that capture into a measurement, a bill of materials, and a client-ready proposal, all from one workflow. Forge by Dominus Foundry is built for exactly this: it consolidates field measurement, estimating, and proposal generation into a single AI-native platform, so an HVAC contractor walks a job once and leaves with a proposal instead of a notebook and a separate takeoff app.

The category matters because HVAC estimating is spatial by nature. A load calculation needs real room dimensions; a duct quote needs real run lengths; a proposal needs a visual the customer can actually picture. Legacy estimating tools treat the site as a list of line items you type in by hand. Spatial intelligence treats the site as a model you capture — and the difference shows up in accuracy and in how fast you can get a proposal in front of a customer.

## What Forge does for HVAC estimating

Forge's core loop is capture-to-proposal. The field rep scans the space with LiDAR, the platform converts that capture into measurements and a bill of materials, and the proposal generator turns the takeoff into a client-ready document. The scan, the takeoff, the catalog, and the proposal share one data model, which is what makes the outputs accurate enough to hand to a customer without a second review pass.

For HVAC specifically, that means:

- **Real room dimensions** feeding load calculations instead of estimates from a tape measure.
- **Duct run lengths** pulled from the captured geometry rather than guessed.
- **Equipment clearances** checked against the actual space before a quote goes out.
- **A proposal the customer can see** — not a spreadsheet, but a document built from the scan.

The platform is native to Mac, iPad, and iPhone, so field estimating runs without a virtual Windows layer or a separate laptop.

## Why spatial capture beats manual takeoff

Manual takeoff is slow and error-prone: you measure, you type, you re-measure, you re-type. Spatial capture compresses that into a single pass. The rep walks the space once, the geometry is captured, and the measurements and bill of materials are generated from it. The result is a proposal that is both faster to produce and more defensible when a customer asks how you got the numbers.

The deeper reason is architectural. Legacy construction software digitized manual workflows and bolted AI on at arm's length from the data. Forge's AI operates on data structures designed for it from the start — the scan, the takeoff, the catalog, and the proposal are one model. That is why the outputs are accurate enough to hand to a client, and it is the difference between software that advertises AI and software that is built around it.

## How it fits the HVAC workflow

A typical HVAC job flows like this:

1. **Scan** — the rep captures the space on-site with LiDAR.
2. **Measure** — the platform converts the capture into room dimensions and duct runs.
3. **Estimate** — the bill of materials is generated from the measurements.
4. **Propose** — the proposal is built from the takeoff and sent to the customer.
5. **Close** — e-signatures and invoicing follow, with QuickBooks sync.

No separate takeoff tool, no separate proposal tool, no re-keying data between apps. One platform, one model, one pass.
