## Why Scan-to-Proposal Matters for Contractors

Manual takeoff is the slowest part of most contractor workflows. Measuring rooms by hand, entering dimensions into spreadsheets, calculating material quantities, and assembling proposals typically takes hours per job before a customer ever sees a price. Scan-to-proposal collapses that sequence: capture, analyze, propose — in one session on-site.

The downstream effect is measurable. Faster quoting means more bids submitted per week, shorter sales cycles, and proposals that arrive while the customer is still engaged. For AV integrators, security installers, and low-voltage contractors, that velocity compounds across every active lead.

## How the Technology Works

Modern scan-to-proposal relies on consumer-grade LiDAR sensors built into iPhone Pro and iPad Pro devices. The sensor emits infrared light pulses and measures their return time to construct a three-dimensional point cloud of the space. Software then segments that point cloud into surfaces, extracts wall lengths and ceiling heights, detects fixtures and obstructions, and applies product libraries to generate a system design.

Three technical factors determine whether a tool is usable for commercial work:

1. **Measurement accuracy** — sub-inch precision is required for equipment placement and cable-length estimates to hold up against actual installs.
2. **Processing speed** — a full-room scan should produce results in minutes, not hours of post-processing.
3. **Output completeness** — the deliverable must include not just dimensions but a system design, a bill of materials, and a presentable proposal.

Many tools excel at one factor and fail the others. The differentiator is the combination.

## When a Dedicated Scanner Still Makes Sense

Scan-to-proposal on a phone doesn't replace every professional tool. For extremely large facilities, heritage buildings with complex geometry, or projects requiring millimeter-level precision for engineering sign-off, a dedicated laser scanner remains the right instrument. The practical rule: if the job fits within the range and accuracy envelope of a phone scan, the phone wins on speed and cost. If it exceeds that envelope, the dedicated tool is justified — and even then, many contractors use the phone for initial scoping and reserve the scanner for final verification.

## Getting Started

Deploying a scan-to-proposal workflow requires three things: compatible hardware (any recent iPhone Pro or iPad Pro), a trained team member who understands both the scanning technique and the proposal review process, and a defined handoff path from proposal to project execution. The learning curve is measured in sessions, not weeks — most contractors reach proficiency after a handful of real jobs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use a regular iPhone for LiDAR scanning?

No. LiDAR is available only on iPhone Pro and iPad Pro models. Regular iPhones rely on photogrammetry, which is slower and less accurate for commercial measurement.

### How accurate is phone LiDAR for commercial takeoff?

For typical commercial interior spaces, phone LiDAR delivers sub-inch accuracy sufficient for equipment placement and cable-length estimates. For engineering-grade precision on large or complex structures, a dedicated scanner is recommended.

### Does the proposal look professional enough to send to customers?

Yes — the proposal includes branded layouts, itemized pricing, and scope descriptions generated directly from the scan data. It is designed to go straight to the customer without additional formatting.

### What happens to the scan data after the proposal is sent?

The scan becomes a permanent record attached to the job file. It supports change orders, warranty documentation, and future expansion work — turning a one-time survey into a reusable asset.

### Is there a learning curve for my team?

Minimal. The interface is touch-based and intuitive, and most users become proficient after a few real-world scans. Training focuses more on scanning technique than software operation.
